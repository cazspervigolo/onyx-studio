# Melbourne Barbershop Website Blueprint
### Evidence-based winning formula for a new barbering business
*Researched 12 July 2026. Method: 20 Melbourne barber businesses analysed — 10 market leaders (selected on awards, longevity, multi-location scale, and review volume) and 10 with weak or broken digital presences. Every site was fetched and its structure recorded in DOM order; two were verified visually in a live browser. Sources listed at the bottom.*

## PART 2 — WHAT THE WINNERS HAVE THAT THE LOSERS DON'T

Ranked by how cleanly the pattern separates the two cohorts:

1. **An owned custom domain.** 10/10 winners; 0/6 Group-A strugglers (wixsite/square.site/gettimely subdomains or none). Caveat from Group B: Renard's (4.9★, no website) proves reputation alone can carry a shop — the owned-domain pattern separates winners from *broken* presences. The website's job is to **capture and scale** demand that word-of-mouth generates, not to create it.
2. **A one-click path from hero to a live booking calendar.** Every winner's primary CTA links directly to Fresha / Square / GetTimely / Acuity. Strugglers: phone-only (Alpha MC), buried (Fade Factory), or nothing (Renard's).
3. **Social proof stated as a number, above the fold.** Empire leads with "4.9 Star Rating, more than 5,000+ Reviews" before you scroll. The Barber Club shows its 2026 award badge + 6 testimonials. Ikigai shows an award wall + Google reviews. Strugglers show nothing — or fake it (Ab's).
4. **Complete, consistent contact info.** Winners: address 9/10, clickable phone 7/10, hours, per-location cards. Strugglers: missing addresses, placeholder phones, contradictory hours.
5. **Local-SEO-loaded titles and headlines.** "Best Barbershop in Melbourne | Empire Barbershop – Oakleigh"; Studio Eighty4 stuffs suburb keywords into its title. Strugglers have generic or missing titles.
6. **Real photography of their own shop and cuts.** All winners. Strugglers use stock or nothing.
7. **Pricing transparency.** 6/10 winners publish full price lists (the premium minority deliberately withholds); Group-A strugglers mostly hide or omit.
8. **Booking platform of choice: Fresha dominates Melbourne** (Empire, Kings Domain, Barber Club, Ikigai + all the 5,000-review marketplace giants). Square and GetTimely are viable; the platform matters less than the one-click path — but Fresha's marketplace listing is itself a discovery + review-accumulation channel. (Balance: the marketplace also surfaces competitors next to your brand, and the free tier monetizes via new-client fees and card processing — factor that into margins.)

### The universal page order (observed across winners)

```
[Trust strip — optional: "Rated 5 stars by thousands of clients"]
[NAV: logo · services · location(s) · phone · BOOK NOW button]
[HERO: real photo of shop/cut
  → headline claiming local identity ("Melbourne's top rated barbershop")
  → proof line (rating + review count or award)
  → PRIMARY CTA "Book Your Haircut" + secondary "Call Us Now"]
[Services + prices]
[Why us / story — years, credentials, awards]
[Gallery — real work]
[Testimonials / embedded Google reviews]
[Location(s): address, map, hours, parking]
[Final booking CTA]
[Footer: socials, contact, policies]
```

**The answer to "image then CTA?":** yes — the dominant winner pattern is *photo hero → identity headline → quantified proof → booking CTA*, all above the fold. The CTA is always a booking verb: **"Book Your Haircut"** (Empire), **"MAKE A BOOKING"** (Kings Domain), **"BOOK NOW"** (Ikigai, Studio Eighty4, All The Kings Men), **"BOOK A CUT"** (Uncle Rocco's), **"Book Online"** (Beef's). Never "Submit", never "Contact us".

## PART 4 — THE BUILD BLUEPRINT (hand this to the builder model)

**Context for the builder:** single-location new barbershop launch, Melbourne, Australia. Goal: maximum bookings from day one. Design mobile-first (working assumption: booking traffic is mobile-dominant — verify in analytics after launch). Note the name-collision warning at the top of this document — confirm final brand name before generating copy, and before committing to any name: check the IP Australia trade-mark register and `.com.au` domain availability.

### 4.1 Required inputs before build
- Final brand name (see collision warning), logo, brand photos (real shop — no stock)
- Street address, suburb, phone, opening hours, Instagram
- Booking platform account and link — **recommend Fresha** (dominant in Melbourne, free tier, marketplace = extra discovery and a review engine). Square Appointments if they want unified POS; GetTimely also common locally.
- Service list with prices and durations

### 4.2 Page architecture (single page + optional service pages later)
Build exactly this order (matches Part 2 winner pattern):
1. **Sticky nav:** logo · Services · The Studio · Visit · phone (tel: link) · `Book Now` button (accent style)
2. **Hero:** full-bleed real photo; H1 = local identity claim containing "barber" + suburb (e.g. "‹Suburb›'s sharpest barbershop"); proof line under it (once reviews exist: "4.9★ from 200+ clients"; at launch: barber credentials/years); primary button `Book Your Haircut` → booking URL; secondary `Call Us` → tel:. Address + today's hours in small text at hero bottom.
3. **Services & prices:** every service with price and duration; one featured signature service visually differentiated.
4. **Proof section:** embedded Google reviews widget (or 3 named testimonials), award/press badges when earned. *Never fabricate — see Ab's cautionary example.*
5. **Gallery:** 4–8 real photos (cuts + interior).
6. **Story:** 2–3 sentences, barber credentials, years of experience.
7. **Visit:** address + Google Maps embed/link, clickable phone, hours table (highlight today), parking/transport line.
8. **Final CTA band:** "Three ways to book" — online / call / walk-in.
9. **Footer:** socials, ABN, hours summary.

### 4.3 Non-negotiable implementation specs
- **CTA wording:** booking verbs only ("Book Your Haircut" / "Book a Chair"). One primary action per screen. Booking link opens the platform in a new tab; on mobile it must be one tap.
- **SEO:** `<title>` = "Barber ‹Suburb› | ‹Name› — Book Online"; meta description with suburb + booking promise; **`HairSalon` JSON-LD** (the correct schema.org type; there is no "Barbershop" type) with NAP, geo, and `openingHoursSpecification`; single H1; image alt text with service/suburb terms.
- **Google Business Profile (own workstream, not an afterthought):** claim GBP before launch; categories "Barber shop"; NAP identical to the site; attach the booking link; upload photos weekly; respond to every review; seed Q&A. For "barber near me" searches the map pack drives more bookings than the website itself — the site and GBP feed each other.
- **Performance:** static or lightly-templated site, WebP images, lazy-load below fold, < 1MB initial payload, Lighthouse mobile ≥ 90.
- **Maintainability:** all owner-editable facts (phone, address, hours, booking URL, prices) in ONE config file or CMS collection — losers rot because updating them is hard (see Yours Truly's contradictory hours).
- **Accuracy checks before launch:** every phone number dials, every link resolves, hours appear in exactly one source of truth, zero placeholder text. (This is the entire 5-point category the strugglers fail.)

### 4.4 Post-launch review engine (what actually separates winners)
The 5,000-review shops got there by process, not luck. Two distinct channels — don't blend them:
1. **Google reviews are the priority** — they drive map-pack discovery. QR code at the counter → Google review link; ask at checkout; owner replies to every review (keeps recency fresh).
2. Booking platform auto-sends review requests after each appointment (Fresha does this natively) — these accumulate **on Fresha's marketplace**, from Fresha-booked clients only. Valuable for marketplace ranking, but they are not Google reviews.
3. Update the hero proof line as counts grow ("4.9★ from 50 / 200 / 1,000 clients") and label the source (Google vs Fresha).
4. Post 2–3 cuts weekly to Instagram; embed the feed.

### 4.5 Measurement
- Track clicks on Book / Call buttons (simple analytics events).
- Weekly: bookings from "online" source vs walk-in/phone (platform dashboards report this).
- Suggested goal (an assumption to tune, not an industry norm): ≥ 60% of bookings online within 3 months.
- Watch review recency: aim for at least a few fresh Google reviews every month.

## Cross-validation
This blueprint was independently reviewed by a second model (Claude Opus), which re-verified the Onyx Studio name collision and Fresha's native review prompts against live sources, validated the core winner/loser patterns, and required the following adjustments — all adopted in this version:
1. Corrected schema markup to `HairSalon` (no "Barbershop" type exists on schema.org).
2. Softened the review-volume claim (proxy metric, partially circular with cohort selection).
3. Added the Renard's caveat: websites capture and scale demand; they don't create reputation.
4. Split Fresha-marketplace reviews from Google reviews and made Google the priority channel.
5. Reweighted the matrix: Google Business Profile promoted to its own 10-pt category; review recency and accessibility now scored; illustrative scores labelled as estimates (speed/accessibility were not instrumented).
6. Flagged the ≥60% online-booking target and mobile-dominance assumption as goals/assumptions, not cited norms.
7. Added Fresha commoditization caveat and an IP Australia trade-mark check to pre-launch due diligence.
Opus's overall verdict: "High [confidence] as a build brief… A builder model can proceed from it today provided the name collision is resolved first."
